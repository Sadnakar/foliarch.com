Chapter I  ·  February 1978

The Winter of 1978

Bahnhofstrasse, Zürich — On risking an entire balance sheet on a single handshake.

By the third week of February, snow had banked waist-high against the iron railings along the Limmat, and Henrik had less than forty-eight hours before the credit line in Rotterdam expired. In the back booth of Café Sprüngli, he laid a single sheet of onion-skin telex paper between two espresso cups—not a formal charter party, only a hand-penciled freight schedule signed in blue fountain ink.

“If the North Sea ice holds through March, we lose the deposit and the vessel,” he recalled forty years later, tapping the ash from his cigarette before the studio microphone. “If it breaks early, we own the corridor.”

He did not cable the board in Stockholm until the train had already crossed the Rhine. In the margin of his pocket ledger that evening, beneath a column of Swiss franc conversions, he wrote a single line that would govern every acquisition the family made for the next four decades: “Never syndicate conviction; share only the harvest.”

Three weeks later, the ice cleared six days ahead of the meteorological forecast. The first cargo docked at dawn, clearing the company’s entire debt before noon.
